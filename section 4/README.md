## Fixed-base rectangles for Proposition 4.21

`SignedShears` constructs the automorphisms on the whole signed RV sort by changing the positive value section. It proves that the restriction to positive leading terms is the specified linear shear and that each old value fiber is fixed pointwise. `DefinableValueFrames` realizes finite affine avoidance from ordinary first-order saturation over the base. The finite disjoint pivot argument in `ValueStratification` gives value frames with an independent point in each projected domain.

`GeneralizedEuler.Section4.canonical_rectangles` in `CanonicalRectangles.lean` combines those results with Section 2 fixed-base quantifier elimination and residue–value separation. For any definable RV set, the positive part has a finite disjoint partition with explicit rectangle bijections. The value domains are definable over the base value subspace and the residue tests come from definable sets over the base residue parameters. The bijection assembles a point by multiplying by monomials with intercept representatives chosen using the section. A section carrying the base values into the base RV parameters is required when passing to definable morphisms. `FrameComparison` derives containment of the whole affine hull, the matrix identities for a refinement, and the rank inequality from one independent point. `FiniteScissors` proves the class formula for finite definable partitions of actual RV objects.

`LexicographicTags` reduces finite-to-one structure maps to a finite sum of definable subsets in the stated degree. `CanonicalChartMaps` verifies the fixed-base graph of the positive chart. `CommonValueRefinement` stratifies intersections of disjoint value partitions. `ValuePadding` computes the degree shift in the concrete value semiring. `RelativeResidues` and `LostCoordinates` identify the corresponding unrestricted residue coordinates. `GraphProjection` proves full rank and injectivity on normalized residues for an injective projection of a nonempty graph rectangle. For the height proof, `WholeFiberRelation` constructs the fixed-base formula for a whole-fiber match and `HeightDrop` proves strict rank drop for the remaining value strata.

`PositivePresentations` combines enumeration and sign changes to express every object class as a finite sum of positive identity-labelled objects. `RectangleGeneration` computes the actual RV class as a sum of products in `GeneralizedEuler.Section4.positive_partition_class`. `RefinedChartTest` computes the pullback of the coarse residue test on a finer value hull. `ResidueScissors` gives finite partition, product and isomorphism equalities inside the residue semiring itself.

`ValueDescent` descends the weight of a single value piece through all five tagged relations. In `ValueFactorMap` the weight is the actual RV pullback class, assuming definability of the pullback and the single-piece lifting conclusion of Lemma 4.12. `RectangleMap` proves balance on finite value pieces and constructs the homomorphism on the tensor product of the concrete factor semirings. `ValueDegrees` proves the rank and point-factor decomposition for single value pieces.

`GeneralizedEuler.Section4.canonical_rectangle_surjective` in `CanonicalSurjectivity` proves surjectivity of the concrete map. It uses the fixed-base QE and saturation hypotheses, the signed value interpretation and the single-piece lifting assertion of 4.12.

`ResidueRefinement` proves that subdividing a chart from rank r to rank s multiplies its residue class by the class of r−s unrestricted positive units. `equal_chart_sums` in `ChartIndependence` proves equality of the assigned sums for any two finite measurable chart families with disjoint carriers and the same union. It stratifies all value supports simultaneously and applies finite scissors to the normalized residues on each stratum. `ProjectedRVChart` computes the carrier of a projected chart. `ProjectedPieceWeights` proves that two injective projections into the same ambient degree have equal normalized residue classes, given the bases selected by the full-rank minors.

`CanonicalChartCover` supplies measurable charts with nonempty residue factors for every fixed-base positive definable set. `GraphPieceTensors` constructs bases on both sides of each graph cell from injectivity and identifies its two tensors. `positive_bijection_weights` in `PositiveValuation` applies this result to the packed graph of an arbitrary fixed-base positive bijection. `PositiveAdditivity` proves finite additivity. `PositiveObjectCover` retains the defining bijections for every tag and sign cell of the original object. `LocalRVMeasure` proves that the sum assigned to a general object is independent of the presentation and is preserved by fixed-base definable bijections. `ObjectTensorWeights` constructs this invariant from the positive chart valuation.


`ArbitraryRectangleWeight` proves the formula for a rectangle whose value domain has smaller rank than its ambient degree. It uses finite value subdivisions and transfers the lost coordinates to the residue factor. `NormalRectangleCover` and `CanonicalObjectWeights` give actual fixed-base bijections between the pieces of a positive object and normalized rectangles. `RectangleMultiplicativity` proves that the assigned weight of a Cartesian product is the product of the assigned weights. `rvTensorAssignment` in `TensorDescent` descends the assignment through all five concrete RV relations. Its chart and rectangle cover hypotheses are supplied by `canonical_admits_charts` and `canonical_object_rectangle_cover`. `SignedTensorFactors` identifies the two placements of the finite-value generator with the rank-one positive-unit class and the degree-one signed value point.

**Graph proof of Proposition 4.21.** `GeneralizedEuler.Section4.proposition_4_21` proves that `balancedRectangleMap` is bijective. Its hypotheses are the fixed-base point property of Remark 4.1, the signed value interpretation and pullback/lifting assertions of Lemma 4.12, the RV-coordinate and power laws, fixed-base QE, base representatives, and value and RV saturation. No rectangle comparison or inverse identity is supplied as an assumption.

`FiniteBaseFibers` chooses base representatives throughout a finite value image. `FixedResidueImages` uses shears and rank-zero charts to control the positive image of a residue object. `ResidueUnitCover` then divides each fiber by base representatives, and `ResidueFactorAssignment` recovers its original RES class in the tensor. `TaggedValueClasses` and `SignedValueGeneration` split arbitrary signed value objects by component and sign. `PureFactorAssignments` computes the weights of both types of generators. The inverse identity extends from these generators to every tensor.

## Question 4.16

The manuscript proves semiring injectivity in every degree over the fixed base. `degree_zero_value_injective` in `DegreeZeroInclusion` proves degree zero for the actual value-factor map. Counting underlying points by extended natural cardinality respects the concrete RV relations. On a degree-zero lift this counts precisely the finite tagged value object.

In degree one, `degree_one_value_injective` in `DegreeOneInclusion` proves that equal images under the actual value-factor homomorphism imply equal tagged value classes. Its residue input is the usual additive, definable-bijection-invariant Euler characteristic of unary residue-field sets, with value −1 on the open positive ray. `ResidueEulerMeasure` derives its use on the RV unit line from fixed-base residue–value separation.

`TaggedLiftRealization` realizes an equality of lift classes by a fixed-base definable bijection of their original tagged unions, in every degree. `TaggedMatchGraph` tests both directions of a fiber match on each pair of tags. `SignedWholeMatches` proves that independent value labels are matched. In degree one, `UnaryRemainders` proves that the remaining labels form finite sets of individually definable points. `SelectedSharpFibers` restricts the original bijection to precisely those fibers. `FiniteFiberEuler` divides by fixed-base representatives and compares the resulting finite unions of positive residue lines, with multiplicity. This gives the missing label counts.

`groupified_value_inclusion` in `GeometricValueInclusion` constructs a left inverse on the groupifications of the concrete RV and value semirings using Proposition 4.21 and the graded residue Euler polynomial of Lemma 4.22. On a residue class of degree `i` and Euler characteristic `a`, tensored with a value class `y`, the formula is `(-1)^i a [e]^i y`. The theorem also proves bijectivity after groupification when the residue Euler polynomial gives the classification in Remark 4.8.

For tagged presentations by padded boxes, `maximal_box_vectors_of_lift_equality` starts with equality in the RV semiring and proves equality of the maximal vectors of scale counts. It applies to any finite list of nonnegative thresholds in the base. `IntervalScales` constructs the convex subspaces and `IntervalScaleBijection` gives the two-slope, fixed-base definable map between intervals of the same scale. `ScaleAvoidance` realizes a coordinate outside the sum of the parameter span and the preceding scale. Distinct scalar fractions of a larger width belong to distinct cosets. `ValueBoxes` uses these choices to attain all thresholds at a single tuple. `BoxFactorMatching` constructs a matching of factors from the cumulative scale inequalities. The proof allows repeated scales and padding points.

`BoxMultiplicity.compare_by_maximal_types` proves the positive comparison for finite partially ordered box types satisfying the extraction, duplication, and face relations of the manuscript. It restricts to the common downward closure, equalizes multiplicities in decreasing dimension, and uses both Euler characteristics to recover the point and single-ray multiplicities. `BoxProductCuts` expands coordinate cuts, retaining the two distinguished summands and all remainder types. `IntervalScaleCuts` and `RaySquareScissors` prove the one-coordinate and two-ray cuts in the concrete value semiring. Instantiating the finite ordered types and their product cuts with all padded boxes remains part of the all-degree proof.

`SimplexBox` proves the fixed-base simplex-to-box step in the semilinear decomposition. On a positive tuple the map has coordinates `yᵢ = ∑ⱼ min(xᵢ,xⱼ)`. Its largest coordinate is the sum of the input coordinates. Lean checks bijectivity, fixed-base definability, and equality of the scissor classes in the original degree. The general reduction from a semilinear set to these simplex fibers still requires the polyhedral decomposition.

## Consequences of Question 4.16

The approved manuscript adds Corollary 4.22a on cancellation by a residue object, the degree-one obstruction to an additive retraction, and the classification of products of bounded open positive RV annuli by their multisets of width scales.

`no_additive_value_retraction` proves the degree-one obstruction for the actual homogeneous value and RV semigroups. In `PositiveResidueLineCut` the two residue-field term maps send a positive residue `x` to `1+x` and its reciprocal. Their images are disjoint and lie in the original fiber. The scissor identity and value-point cardinality then contradict any additive map sending that fiber back to a singleton. This proof uses the residue-field terms and the fixed-base value lift and requires no Euler characteristic.

For Corollary 4.22a, `euler_zero_residue_not_injective` distinguishes a singleton from its tagged double in every value degree. It uses the dimension–Euler classification of Remark 4.8 to show that their products with an Euler-zero residue object coincide. For the converse, `cancel_residue_euler` proves equality of both integer Euler invariants when the nonzero residue factor is cancelled. `maximal_box_vectors_of_residue_product_equality` proves that equality after multiplication by any nonempty residue object forces equality of maximal vectors. Remark 4.1 supplies old representatives on each of its finitely many value fibers. The proof allows the bijection to change the residue coordinates. Finishing the converse requires the general box decomposition and the concrete box-union comparison used in Question 4.16.

`bounded_rv_annuli_iff_same_scales` proves the classification of products of bounded positive RV annuli over the fixed base. A definable bijection forces equal arities and a permutation matching every interval width by scale. Reading the attained quotient ranks at consecutive cutoffs gives the multiplicity of each scale. The converse lifts a coordinate permutation and the interval bijections of Lemma 4.12. This includes the empty product.

`two_annuli_distinct_before_groupification` proves the accompanying example for widths with `c • a < b` for every positive scalar `c`. The boxes of widths `(a,b)` and `(b,b)` lift to distinct RV scissor classes. In the value Grothendieck ring the open-ray cut identifies each bounded interval with minus the unary point, so both rectangle classes map to the square of the lifted point. `objectClass_eq_iff_definably_equivalent` realizes an equality of concrete RV object classes in the same degree by a definable bijection between the two original carriers.
